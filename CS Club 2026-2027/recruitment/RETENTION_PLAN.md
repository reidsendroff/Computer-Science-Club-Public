# Retention Plan

## Why this exists

Last year's fast jump into advanced algorithms coincided with a decline from about 15 members to 3–4. This plan changes pacing, access, and feedback rather than blaming learners.

## Leading practices

- Ten shared Java meetings before specialization
- Short instruction and hands-on majority
- Runnable finish every week
- Flexible core/support/stretch choices
- Zero-install and paired-device fallbacks
- Weekly anonymous pace and fun signal
- Recovery builds and private rechecks
- Meaningful advanced extensions and rotating member demos

## Aggregate measures

Define the baseline cohort as unique participants across the first three regular meetings. Monthly, record:

- returning active participants ÷ baseline cohort (retention percentage);
- number of first-time participants and returners;
- percentage producing the core runnable evidence;
- anonymous pace distribution (too slow / about right / too fast);
- anonymous fun/usefulness distribution;
- number using recovery and later completing a recheck, in aggregate only.

Do not publish names, attendance logs, individual progress, or small-group breakdowns that could identify a student.

## Reset triggers

Reteach, repeat the checkpoint, or redesign the next shared meeting when fewer than 60% of the privately defined active cohort attend the checkpoint, fewer than 80% of checkpoint participants complete all core evidence, 30% or more of valid pace responses select “too fast,” response coverage is insufficient, or multiple comments name the same barrier. Apply the exact rules in [Readiness Gates](../curriculum/READINESS_GATES.md); keep support in the same room and recheck within two attended meetings. Presidents assign one change and review its effect the following week.
